# Explainer purpose and hero selection — 30 October 2026

This is an editorial follow-up to the [29-scene assessment](explainer-stack-rank-2026-10-30.md), not a new comprehension study or a claim that the product is deployed with a new gallery. Source catalog at `59899dc` contained 29 explainers; the reviewed public export held six. The assessment read source, default cases, alternate endings and contact sheets; the [rubric](../EXPLAINER-RUBRIC.md) now distinguishes **lesson quality** (Q/P/V/B/G/E, /24) from **hero fit** (J/R/C, /12). Hero scores below are selection judgments about what a prospective author can learn from an example, not measurements of demand. A niche lesson can be excellent without being a homepage hero.

## Candidates to cut as standalone *lessons*, not to delete as code

- **`layout-direction` — strongest cut candidate.** Two steps establish RTL panel placement and an LTR field override, but no reader problem or consequence; it scored 17/24. Put the example and screenshots in the stage/direction reference, with its API and regression tests intact. Reconsider a lesson only when an actual layout failure and repair can be shown. It is already absent from the six-scene public export.
- **`glyph` — demote or split.** Its final Inter/`textLength` diagram changes the question from glyph shaping to diagram-label width; the 18/24 assessment records that detour. Preserve its glyph/outline tests and reference demonstration, then either end the lesson on the original comparison or give label fitting its own bounded guide. It is already absent from the public export.
- **`fork` — remove from a reader-first gallery, keep its provenance.** Its own engine history and build costs serve contributors, but repeat material better housed in `docs/UPSTREAM.md` and `docs/ENGINE.md`; repeated architecture pictures ask the reader to detect small changes. Do not erase the source or evidence until an author-facing provenance guide carries the distinct information. It is already absent from the public export.
- **`font-metrics` — keep as a narrow author reference, not a hero.** It illustrates natural measurements and font cuts; give it a genuine node-placement puzzle and show the measured box widths if reinstated as a lesson. Its 18/24 is a rewrite signal, not evidence the technique lacks users.

**Not cut on present evidence:** `quotient-familial`, `gtfs`, `wcag-map` and the other lower-ranked subject scenes teach different, sourced questions. Their pacing/density/ending problems warrant edits, not deletion. `agentic-mermaid` demonstrates a distinct warning → typed repair → clean snapshot and remains a useful contributor example. Never infer that a low rubric score means the underlying topic or language is unimportant.

Cutting a catalog entry is a separate decision from deleting its source, test or documented API example. Before changing the source catalog, move any unique instruction to a reference, keep direct tests for examples no longer in the built gallery, check links and curated posters, and re-run the full source/story checks. The current six-scene public export does **not** change as a result of this note.

## Source of the new criterion

The design observations came from [Lantern's live seven-step map](https://lantern.ivar.workers.dev/?scene=lantern), official [3Blue1Brown text adaptations](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/linear-transformations/) and its [SoME1 editorial criteria](https://www.3blue1brown.com/blog/some1/), [Manim Community's example guidance](https://docs.manim.community/en/stable/contributing/docs/examples.html) and [manim-web's Fourier and matrix examples](https://manim-web.hugos29.dev/examples). They suggest questions about motivation, persistent identity, consequential variation and useful motion; they do not independently certify any scene's technical truth or reader learning. An informal prediction or author-adaptation session should record actual responses before anyone claims the new hero works better.
