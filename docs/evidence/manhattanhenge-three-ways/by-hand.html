Why Manhattanhenge falls on the evenings it does

Twice a year the setting Sun lines up with Manhattan's cross-streets, on two consecutive evenings each time. The drawing below computes where the Sun is, evening by evening, and shows what picks those four dates out of the year.

    What this page computes, and what it assumes

    Sun positions follow the low-precision method in Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms (chapter 25, about 0.01°), with refraction from Saemundsson's formula at a standard atmosphere. The observer is 5th Avenue at 42nd Street (40.7534° N, 73.9807° W) and the street bearing is the Hayden Planetarium's 299.1°. The model is in this page's source; every number drawn comes from it. Its crossing instants fall inside the minutes the planetarium published for 2026 (as reported by CBS News and others; the planetarium's own page refuses automated readers).

    What it does not know is how high the visible western horizon sits from a given street. That one input decides which evening gets which name, which is why it is a setting rather than a fact. The buildings in the drawing are a schematic 30 m wide street seen from 2 km, not a survey.
