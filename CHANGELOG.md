# Changelog

## Unreleased: 0.2.0-explainers.0

Newest first; all on 24–26 October 2026.

- **Videos paced by their motion**: the film no longer holds each picture under its caption at 160 words a minute, which left 82% of every video still and ran pen-and-pencil to 2 min 16 s. Each step plays its motion, then rests at most 2 s (`explain/film.ts`) while the view pushes in by up to 2.5%; the caption goes to the subtitles and the transcript. A standalone video made without this project showed the way: something drawing almost all the time, and on screen only a numbered title and one short line.
  - Steps take a `line`: a few words under the title, on the page and in the video. The heading carries a circled step number. The caption footer and `--footer` are gone.
  - `s.focus(target)` moves the engine camera so a target fills the stage below the heading; the heading, number and line stay put on screen, over a paper band zoomed content passes under, and draw over everything. `s.carry(m, box)` moves an object on instead of clearing it. `s.dimension`, `s.pin` and `s.callout` say a value on the drawing, sized for the screen under a zoomed camera. `s.draw(m, { tool })` draws stroke by stroke with a pen or pencil on the tip, splitting a many-stroke path first and sweeping short strokes together so none appears at once.
  - `explain check` fails a film still for over 3 s at once, or, from 10 s long, paused for over 20% of its motion or resting for over 60% of its length, and text that runs into the heading's line; it reports `pacing` and `cardsAtRest`. `explain filmstrip` samples the video mid-motion for review, and the agent skill has a review checklist.
  - Every explainer has a line on every step; `fork` shows its step's new timing beside the old hold, and `pen-and-pencil` uses the camera, the pen, annotations and carry in place of cards and a magnified copy.
  - Renderer: `toSvgString` takes a `view` for an exporter's own framing. PNGs and video frames let the stage's inner `<svg>` overflow: resvg 2.6.2 panics (geom.rs, unwrap on None) on a translucent element inside a nested svg that clips its viewport while something reaches past it, as the paper does under a zoomed camera.

- **Pen-and-pencil explainer**: `scenes/explainers/pen-and-pencil` models the method of [pele-art-diagrams](https://pele-art-diagrams.vercel.app/) as described in [rheos/secondunit-videos' study](https://github.com/rheos/secondunit-videos/blob/main/references/pele-diagrams/README.md) of it: real charts laid out by pele, read back as marks, and drawn by a simulated hand inside a made-up world. Six steps on a timeline of its own (Agentic Mermaid stands in for pele, which the collection does not use, and has no sankey): the Mermaid text and the page's four plates; the layout; the marks traced in blue construction pencil; a pen modelled as a damped spring (8 Hz, damping 0.6) chasing a point along each line with tremor; scenery drawn far to near only on paper the chart, its words and the card do not own; then the chart as objects (planks, tags on strings, a rope rail) and Hershey words last. Pen pressure drifts and pencils dull and are re-sharpened every 60 strokes. Two cases draw the plate with two seeds. Hershey Simplex comes from Paul Bourke's public-domain transcription; `test/explain-pen-and-pencil.test.ts` holds the hand, the hatching, region ownership and word fitting to properties, and the fidelity test holds the scene's numbers to the model. It is not the page's code, which carries no licence.
- **Faster player**: Next no longer re-runs the scene to draw the step's resting picture; the animation's own run carries on undrawn to that frame and draws it afresh. While the reader is on a step, the scene runs ahead to the next step's first frame and waits there, so Next starts at once. Each step's resting picture is kept per case, so returning to a step copies it instead of re-running the scene. On a 4× slowed CPU at 390 px: tap to motion 220–480 ms → about 100–215 ms, the longest freeze during Next 230–430 ms → 50–170 ms, a step already visited 180–490 ms → under 100 ms.

- **Explainers use the engine features** (`4d3c492`, `9b65f93`; branch `claude/intelligent-mendel-8djvkt`, PR #2): seven explainers, plus the Lantern explainer's camera focus, were rebuilt on the engine features below. Counts on screen come from the model or from a transform's recorded evidence, never from the caption. Not every feature has a user yet. One explainer each uses `TransformDiff`, `Underline`, `stateView`, `plot`, `lineGraph`, `saveState`/`Restore` and `code.span`/`code.token`. None uses `TransformMatching` with its own key, `Axes.bar`, `areaUnder`, a standalone `NumberLine`, a bare `Brace` with `tip()` or `putAtTip()`, `code.lineSpan`, `TransformDiff`'s `hunks` option, `diffLines`, `diffTokens`, `parseUnifiedDiff`, `hunkHeader`, `restore()` or `viewMismatch` yet. [The reference](docs/REFERENCE.md#engine-features) names the scenes that use each feature and marks the rest.
  - The explainers ride the camera: `d.focus(ids | 'all', { camera: true, zoom, margin, also })` moves `this.frame` onto the chosen parts, their notes and any native objects in `also` instead of rescaling the artwork (without `camera`, focus enlarges the artwork inside the diagram's box as before, which send-sync, gtfs and fork rely on; parts in focus are described as focused). No point moves, so labels keep their world size and the camera enlarges them; `focus('all')` and `s.clear()` bring the camera home, and the view stays inside the 16 × 9 stage. The heading, cards and code are world objects, so a focus that leaves them out of its box takes them out of view. `check` measures labels on screen (world size × camera zoom) inside the camera's view and judges resting text there: text cut by the view's edge fails, text wholly outside it is off screen; a camera move alone is a new resting picture. The reader's zoom narrows the `viewBox` on top of the author's camera (`ReaderSink` in `app/embed-zoom.ts`) instead of scaling the element: crisp at any zoom, no layout shift, the author's camera keeps playing under it, and Fit is the author's frame; phone close-ups compose on top. The Lantern explainer's `points` step frames Mobject, its note, the circle and its point listing with the camera.
  - `glyph`: `Underline` on the close-up letter and a `BraceLabel` per font's measured width; `SurroundingRectangle` on the widest and narrowest letters' advance cells; a "letterforms" step where `TransformMatchingShapes` pairs every glyph with a smaller copy of itself and none across fonts; the text diff is a real `TransformMatchingText` ("cache miss" to "cache hit") with lines between its pairs, `Cross` on what shrinks and `Underline` on what grows, its counts from `summary()`. The fidelity test holds the counts to an independent letter count and the shape pairings to a direct outline comparison.
  - `mr-cache` (the KV-cache merge request): the diff step morphs coloured native `Code` (token spans from `tools/tokens.ts`, checked in as data) with `TransformDiff`, tints lines and fills its card from the transform's `plan`, and rings the KV lookup with `Circumscribe({ keep: true })`; `SurroundingRectangle` replaces the focus bars. The fidelity test holds its recorded hunks to `git diff --no-index --unified=0`.
  - `state-machine`: a kept `Circumscribe` ring moves to the state `reduceSession()` returns; the reducer row it read is found by key with a code span, boxed with `SurroundingRectangle`, and put back with `saveState`/`Restore` when the next event arrives; each event plays as one `Succession`; the Session card is a `view(this, render)` checked with `assertViewFollowsState`.
  - `seasons`: a "noon" step zooms the camera onto the noon angle with its formula; the year chart is native `Axes` with a 145-sample `plot` of `sunlight()` (the polar-day and polar-night counts evaluate every day), with solstice and equinox guides, and the compare step draws all four places on one axes, replacing its captured xy charts. The fidelity test adds polar-day and polar-night counts.
  - `token-bucket`: a native `Axes` chart of the run beside the gauge, drawn from the same `allow()` calls; each step's requests are one `Succession`; red dots mark refused requests under the one-token line; the readout is a `stateView` synced call by call; the last step overlays the runs at 1, 2 and 4 tokens a second. Assertions on the traced state replace its captured xy charts and their fidelity check.
  - `ring-buffer`: three views of `scene.state` (head, tail and length; the card's values; the waiting push argument), all checked with `assertViewFollowsState`; one `Succession` per step, one of whose members writes the model's answer into the state at its turn; `SurroundingRectangle` on the slot touched; `TransformFromCopy` into and out of slots; in the Full case a copy that stops short, a `Cross` and an unchanged readout. Still no `catch` in `construct()`.

The fidelity test checks that evidence in both cases.
- **Engine features** (branch `claude/intelligent-mendel-8djvkt`, PR #2; rebased onto `19316f3`): zero-dependency additions to `src/` that carry the rule that the animation shows what the program does into code morphs, camera moves, emphasis, charts and readouts. Each is seek-exact: every frame is a pure function of alpha over endpoints fixed at `begin()`, tested against a full run. [The engine map](docs/ENGINE.md) links each guide, and [upstream](docs/UPSTREAM.md#engine-changes) lists them with their tests.
  - Matching and diff-driven transforms (`bc9bbc4`): `TransformMatching(source, target, { key })` pairs drawable parts by key instead of by family position; matched parts morph, the rest shrink out and grow in, carried with their line. `TransformMatchingText` keys glyphs by character, font and weight, longest runs first in reading order; `TransformMatchingShapes` keys by `VMobject.shapeKey()` (the same path up to position and size), which `test/manim.test.ts` holds to manim's `has_same_shape_as` through a case in `tools/oracle/manim_math.py`. `summary()` and `pairs`, `removed` and `added` report the pairing. `new TransformDiff(before, after, { hunks })` morphs one `Code` into another with the diff as the pairing: unchanged lines travel whole to the line the hunks say; in a hunk a removed line pairs with the added line most like it and their shared tokens morph character for character; everything else fades out or grows in; `plan` says what each line did. Given hunks are the truth, and hunks that do not describe the texts throw at `begin()`. `src/diff.ts` adds `diffLines` (hunks with no context; git's output on edits with one minimal diff, held to `git diff --no-index --unified=0` on random edits), `diffTokens` and `tokenize`, `parseUnifiedDiff` (any context size) and `hunkHeader`. Step evidence: `scene.record(kind, data)` appends to the step's evidence, `traceScene` steps carry it as `StepRecord.evidence` only when present (so traces of scenes that record nothing hash as before), matching transforms record their pairing counts and `TransformDiff` its hunks when they begin (a View's internal morph, `view.sync()`, does not record evidence), and `renderText` prints a one-line summary. See [matching](docs/MATCHING.md).
  - Camera (`bc9bbc4`): `scene.frame`, a `Frame`, is a never-drawn mobject whose points are the visible box, so `this.play(this.frame.animate.fit(code, { margin: 0.5 }))`, `.scale(0.5).moveTo(x)` and `.reset()` zoom and pan, seek-safe. SVG and DOM sinks map it to the `viewBox` only; element records are untouched by a camera move. `renderText` and `MotionSink` see through it (screen space), so a camera cut reports as a teleport. `renderElements()` and `toSvgString()` take `cull` (off by default): a leaf whose box, grown by its stroke, leaves under 2 output pixels of the view is left out, and a group only when it clears the view by 0.5 units. The video exporter passes `cull: true` (`4d3c492`): resvg 2.6.2, the latest release, aborts on an element with `opacity` whose visible box is empty, and seasons' noon zoom left one glyph at opacity 0.054 beyond the frame. Culling is a pure function of the state, and kept records keep their identity. See [camera](docs/CAMERA.md).
  - Emphasis (`bc9bbc4`): `SurroundingRectangle`, `Underline`, `Cross`, `Brace` (with `tip()` and `putAtTip()`) and `BraceLabel` fit a mobject's box or a given `Box`, so a span of code lines can be marked. They are built for `Create` (a pen's draw order) and are snapshots of the target's box, as in manim. `Circumscribe` draws an outline around its target and fades it out, or with `keep: true` leaves it for the step's resting picture. See [emphasis](docs/EMPHASIS.md).
  - Graphing (`bc9bbc4`): `NumberLine` (`n2p`, `p2n`) and `Axes` (`c2p`, `p2c`, `originPoint`, `plot`, `lineGraph`, `bar`, `dot`, `verticalLine`, `horizontalLine`, `areaUnder`) chart the scene's own numbers with no build step. Plots are monotone cubics through every sample (no overshoot at clamps); bars stand on y = 0 and morph by index under `Transform`; mapping reads the axes' current points, so it survives shift and rotate. `Create(axes)` with number labels needs `{ runTime: 2 }` or `{ lagRatio: 0.1 }` to pass the motion check. See [graphing](docs/GRAPHING.md).
  - Views (`bc9bbc4`): `view(build)` and `view(scene, render)` return a `View`, a Group whose one child is a picture rebuilt from state. `view.sync()` builds when its play begins (so after any state change, or after the `Succession` members before it) and morphs the current picture into the new one: `TransformMatchingText` by default for text, by-index `Transform` otherwise, or `match: 'shapes' | 'index' | key`. The View keeps its identity, parent and anchor (`place`, scale carried); an unchanged picture moves nothing but still takes its run time; there are no updaters, so seeks still skip. `stateView(scene, keys)` is a `StateReadout` as a View, and `viewMismatch(view, state)`, with `assertViewFollowsState` in `test/lib/view-fidelity.ts`, checks that the picture at every step is what the traced state renders. Of Lantern's panels only `StateReadout` is back in the engine (`src/panels.ts`), because `stateView` builds one; `title` and `CodePanel` stay deleted. See [views](docs/VIEWS.md).
  - Code spans and build-time syntax tokens (`bc9bbc4`): `code.span(line, from, to)`, `code.token(line, text, nth)` and `code.lineSpan(line)` return a `CodeSpan`, a live view of a line's glyphs by source index (UTF-16 code units of the line as written, tabs unexpanded): `setFill` recolours the real text, `box()` is advance cells by the line's ascent and descent (spans of one line line up and tile), and `span.animate` plays on the line. A span is stale after its line's text changes. `Code.copy()` gives the copy its own `lines`. `new Code(lines, { spans })` paints `ColorSpan` colours at construction; `node tools/tokens.ts <file> --lang ts` computes them at build time, with `shiki` 4.5.0 (a devDependency of this checkout) or a fallback lexer for ts/js, python, bash and elixir, and the output is checked-in data. The DOM renderer draws each run of equal-styled glyphs as one `<path>` (it was one element per glyph once a line had two styles): a 20-line block coloured by shiki is 163 elements, where one element per glyph on each mixed line would be 578. The SVG string renderer is unchanged. See [code](docs/CODE.md).
  - Restore, copies and sequencing (`bc9bbc4`): `saveState()`, `restore()` and `new Restore(mobject)` return to a saved state exactly, in points, style and structure. Fields that name a part, such as `Code.lines`, name the mobject's own children after a restore, not the saved copy's detached children, which made code spans draw each restored line twice until a code-span test found it. `copy()` does not carry the saved state. `new TransformFromCopy(source, target)` morphs a copy of `source` into `target`; the copy is taken before anything in the play begins, so `source` can be animated in the same play, in either argument order. `new Succession([...])` runs members one after another, each begun when its turn comes, exactly as consecutive plays would; members may drive the same mobject, `play()`'s overlap check looks through it, and a `lagRatio` given to `play()` leaves it sequential. See [animations](docs/ANIMATIONS.md).
  - New exports from `rayner`: `Frame`, `TransformMatching`, `TransformMatchingShapes`, `TransformMatchingText`, `TransformDiff`, `diffLines`, `diffTokens`, `parseUnifiedDiff`, `tokenize`, `hunkHeader`, `SurroundingRectangle`, `Underline`, `Cross`, `Brace`, `BraceLabel`, `Circumscribe`, `NumberLine`, `Axes`, `View`, `ViewSync`, `view`, `stateView`, `viewMismatch`, `Restore`, `TransformFromCopy`, `Succession` and `StateReadout` (the one panel export), with their option types and `MatchKey`, `MatchSummary`, `LinePlan`, `Evidence`, `Hunk`, `DiffOp`, `Token`, `TokenOp`, `CodeSpan`, `ColorSpan` and `RenderOptions`.
- **Engine fixes** (`bc9bbc4`, the cut-in rule tightened in `53c5add`; branch `claude/intelligent-mendel-8djvkt`): a `StopScene` thrown by a sink now stops the scene for good (`scene.stopped`), so a `construct()` that swallows it in a broad `catch` can no longer emit frames after it, and a seek renders the played frame ([reference](docs/REFERENCE.md#scene)).
